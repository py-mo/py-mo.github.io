Large Concept Models (LCMs): A Paradigm Shift Beyond Token-Level Language Modeling

September 12, 2025

Standard LLMs operate at the token level—chunking text into words or subwords and predicting the next token.
But human thought works differently: we think in concepts, flows, and structures, not just discrete units.

Core Innovation#

  • Semantic-level reasoning: The Large Concept Model (LCM) handles text at the sentence (concept) level instead of tokens, using the SONAR multilingual and multimodal embedding space as its foundation.
  • Language and modality-agnostic abstraction: Concepts are universal—independent of language and format—enabling the same reasoning pipeline for text or speech across 200 languages.

Architectural Variants#

One-Tower Models#

A single encoder-decoder stack directly predicts the next sentence representation in the SONAR space.

  • Simpler design, but challenges in capturing cross-sentence consistency.

Two-Tower Models#

Separate encoders and decoders:

  • Encoder maps input into SONAR embedding space.
  • Decoder predicts the next concept embedding.
  • Provides modularity and allows adaptation across languages and modalities.

Diffusion-Based Models#

Instead of direct prediction, sentence embeddings are generated through iterative denoising steps, akin to diffusion models in vision.

  • Captures richer distributions.
  • Enables higher flexibility in multi-modal reasoning.

Scaling Experiments#

  • LCM-1.6B trained on ~1.3T tokens across 200 languages.
  • LCM-7B scaled to ~2.7T tokens, showing stronger multilingual performance and long-context coherence.
  • Scaling follows trends seen in LLMs: larger LCMs generalize better, but with efficiency gains due to shorter concept sequences.

Key Insights#

  • Efficiency: Concept-level modeling compresses input—reducing sequence length compared to tokens.
  • Coherence: Higher-level abstractions improve sentence-level consistency in long-form text.
  • Flexibility: Modular architecture allows extension to new modalities with minimal retraining.
